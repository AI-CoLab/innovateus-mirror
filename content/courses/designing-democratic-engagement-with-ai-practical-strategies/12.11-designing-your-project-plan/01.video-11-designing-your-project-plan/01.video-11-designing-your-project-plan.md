# Video 11: Designing Your Project Plan

This video explains how to design a project plan by working backward from the decision you need to make. It covers how to sequence different methods across phases and assign clear ownership, and how you can use AI to structure timelines, simulate scenarios, and stress-test your design.

Let's get started.

Designing your plan. Mapping your project plan before you launch, ensuring each phase has a clear purpose, an owner, and a connection to what comes next, is essential. Start by working backward from the decision. What does the decision-maker need to see, in what form, and by when? Then design the steps required to get there. This keeps the process focused on what is actionable, not just what is interesting. Think about how different types of input fit together. A useful approach is to start broad, surfacing issues and gathering perspectives, and then narrow toward shared priorities and concrete recommendations. Use different methods at different stages. Early phases may focus on gathering input from a wide audience, while later phases may involve smaller groups working in more depth. The goal is to match the method to the task at each step. Design for continuity. Engagement should not be a one-off event, but part of an ongoing cycle where each effort builds on what came before and informs what comes next. Connect the process to decision-making. Align each phase to a specific moment where input can influence policy or action. The clearer the connection, the more likely participation will matter. Finally, assign clear ownership. Each phase should have someone responsible with the authority to move it forward. A plan only works if people are accountable for carrying it out.

Using AI to develop your project plan. AI can support the design of your project plan in several practical ways. You can use it to structure the process. Given a goal and timeline, AI can help outline the phases of an engagement, suggesting how to move from input collection to deliberation to decision-making, and identifying where different methods may fit. By simulating different scenarios, such as high participation or delays between phases, AI can help you identify where the process might slow down or break, and adjust your plan before launch. It can support coordination. You can use AI to create checklists or run-of-show documents that keep teams aligned as the engagement unfolds. It can also help you stress-test your design. You can use AI to review your plan from different perspectives—participant, staff, or decision-maker—to identify gaps, unclear transitions, or missing steps. Done well, you are using AI to help you design a clearer, more resilient project plan that connects to real decisions.

\[Visual description]: Title appears: Risks to Watch For.

Risks to watch for. A few risks deserve attention.

\[Visual description]: Text appears: Poorly designed project plans.

First, poorly designed project plans can create bottlenecks that delay or derail the process. If responsibilities are unclear, timelines are unrealistic, or phases are not connected, input can get stuck between collection and decision-making. The best way to prevent this is to assign clear owners and define how each step leads to the next before you launch.

\[Visual description]: Text appears: Overcomplicating the process.

Second, overcomplicating the process can make it harder to manage and harder for participants to follow. Too many stages, tools, or transitions increase the risk of confusion, drop-off, and breakdowns between phases.

\[Visual description]: Title appears: Keep the plan simple while still meeting your goals.

Keep the plan as simple as possible while still meeting your goals.

\[Visual description]: Text appears: Poor decision-making.

Finally, a plan that isn't grounded in decision-making will fail to deliver results. If there is no clear path from input to action, participation may generate activity without impact.

A strong project plan is clear, connected, and accountable, designed from the start to move input all the way through to decisions.
