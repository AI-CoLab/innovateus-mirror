# Resources: Academic Research

###

Finding solutions in academic research Academic research often offers discussions backed by evidence about policy interventions or programs. The challenge is that many journal articles are behind a paywall and can be hard to access without a university login. Here we have listed some aggregators of journal articles that are free to search. We have also included examples of other resources that can similarly lead you to the evidence related to solutions of interest to you.

#### Academic journal aggregators

- [Social Science Research Network (SSRN)](https://www.ssrn.com/index.cfm/en/)

  SSRN is a searchable online library used to share scholarly research. It covers disciplines that include the applied sciences, health sciences, humanities, life sciences, physical sciences, and social sciences. Most papers can be downloaded free of charge.

- [arXiv.org](http://arxiv.org/)

  Operated by Cornell University, arXiv.org hosts more than two million scholarly articles in the fields of physics, mathematics, computer science, quantitative biology, quantitative finance, statistics, electrical engineering and systems science, and economics.

- [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/)

  Papers published on ScienceDirect focus on scientific, technical and health research, with more than 1.4 million articles freely available to read. Accessing the full database requires subscription, or access through an institution such as a university.

- [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/)

  Google Scholar is a free web search engine that indexes a vast array of scholarly research, including journal articles, conference papers, theses and books. Citation data enables you to explore related research and can help you to assess the value of the research contributions.

- [Connected Papers](https://www.connectedpapers.com/)

  Connected Papers creates visual graphs of related research papers. After entering information about one paper, Connected Papers will map its links to other papers. It can be useful for building your understanding of a new field and help you to explore research that has clustered around a specific topic.

#### Further resources

- [Google Books](https://books.google.com/)

  Searching within Google Books will provide a list of books that match your search terms. In many cases you will be able to access a preview of the book as well as be able to find information about where you can borrow or buy the book.

- [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/)

  The Library of Congress is the largest library in the world and the main research arm of the U.S. Congress. It offers a range of general and specialized collections. You can learn more about how to use the Library’s as a research tool [here](https://www.loc.gov/rr/gettingstarted.html).

  Other public and university libraries

  All libraries offer free book search, so it can be worth exploring how they can support your fast field scanning. For example, the New York Public Library has a dedicated [research catalog](https://www.nypl.org/research), and university library catalogs, such as [Harvard’s HOLLIS library system](https://hollis.harvard.edu/primo-explore/search?vid=HVD2\&sortby=rank\&lang=en_US), can be searched to at least identify relevant materials that may be worth exploring further.
