# Resources: Tips for contacting experts

Early in the project

Consult well-connected people who will open doors to others, in addition to being good sources of information themselves.

### Write the letter you want someone else to send!

Use these tips to help you draft an email or letter that experts you turn to can easily forward to others by writing a draft you want them to forward.

- Be brief - aim for no more than three short paragraphs.

- Avoid - starting your email or letter with “My name is…” or “I”. You will sign off with your name and you want to indicate you are more interested in the recipient than yourself.

- Get to the point quickly - state what your email or letter is about and what you want in your first paragraph. Busy people appreciate the time this saves them.

- Be compelling - include a line about the problem you are trying to solve and why it matters.

- Be specific - state clearly what you are asking for. If you are seeking an interview, include a few questions you would ask.

- Keep your ask small - this makes it easier for the person to say ‘yes’ to your request, and chances are they will give you more once you have successfully engaged them.

- Explain how you will use their input - people will want to know where and how their advice will be applied.

- Re-read and revise - allow time to revise the letter or ask a colleague to review it to help you see it through the eyes of the recipient and what would help them to say ‘yes’ to your request.

### Think carefully about who needs to be consulted

Ask yourself who may feel disrespected if you do not inform them of your work, consult with them, or invite them to an early meeting or discussion. Find ways to seek out the perspectives or advice of these people early on in your fast field scanning process. This may include key people inside your organization.

### Busy and important people

Interview these people late in the process, once you have strong knowledge about your subject so you can make the most of what is likely to be the only opportunity you will have to speak with them.
