# Resources: Finding solutions from people

### Finding solutions from people

Informed people are often the fastest shortcut to learning what has been tried, how it got done, and what is working. Here we have listed some ways you can find knowledgeable people in practitioner networks, academia and on social media. Don’t forget you can prompt genAI to help you find knowledgeable people.

### Practitioner networks

People with first-hand experiences of implementing policies or programs similar to those you are involved in are often good sources of knowledge about solutions. While reaching out to individuals in other governments is a logical place to begin, becoming involved in communities of practice can be a good way to continually gain and share knowledge about new and innovative interventions.

Here we have suggested an initial list of networks relevant for state government professionals. Further suggestions can be emailed to hello@innovate-us.org

### Academic and scientific experts

The [Scholars Strategy Network](https://scholars.org/) has been specifically designed to serve as a source of academic experts interested in talking to policymakers. The Network has more than 1800 researchers in 47 states. Combing through relevant documents identified in research aggregators such as Google Scholar and ScienceDirect - see Finding solutions in academic research - is also a good way to find subject matter experts.

Finding experts through social media and the ‘fediverse’

Both legacy and emerging social media platforms can be searched to find authoritative voices on specific topics. [LinkedIn](https://about.linkedin.com/),[ X (previously Twitter)](https://twitter.com/), [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/), [Threads](https://help.instagram.com/788669719351544), [BlueSky](https://bsky.social/about/), and [Mastodon](https://mastodon.social/explore) are just some platforms you can use to find a relevant expert or online community with relevant knowledge. Using a hashtag search engine such as [Hashtagify](https://hashtagify.me/hashtag/summer) can help to identify people who cluster around the specific topic you are most interested in.

Short for ‘federated universe’ the ‘fediverse’ is a series of interconnected networks that allow communication across different social media platforms. It is emerging as a key part of the social media landscape that seeks to offer a more seamless experience and to improve the quality of interactions among online communities.

For more on the fediverse [read this explainer](https://mashable.com/article/what-is-the-fediverse), [watch this video](https://fediverse.info/) and see the [kinds of communities](https://fediverse.info/explore/projects) that are appearing on the fediverse as alternatives to legacy social media platforms.
