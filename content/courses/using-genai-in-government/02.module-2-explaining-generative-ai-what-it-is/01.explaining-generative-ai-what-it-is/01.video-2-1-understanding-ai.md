# Video 2.1 | Understanding AI

The video explains artificial intelligence (AI) and generative AI (GenAI) in simple terms. GenAI's ability to understand, create and synthesize knowledge based on training data is described. Examples demonstrate GenAI's capabilities in generating text, images, and engaging in multi-modal interaction.

Artificial intelligence refers to computer systems that perform tasks typically associated with human intelligence such as classification, prediction or content generation.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Artificial Intelligence (AI) is technology that enables computers and machines to simulate human learning, comprehension, problem solving, decision making, creativity, and autonomy.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Scientist John McCarthy coined the term “artificial Intelligence” at the Dartmouth Workshop in 1956.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: a black and white picture of John McCarthy sitting behind a computer.

The term “artificial intelligence” was coined in the 1950s, and while it captured the imagination, the word “intelligence” has caused some confusion. AI tools are not conscious. They are computer systems trained on large amounts of data to recognize patterns and generate outputs in response to the instructions we give them.

*\[Visual description]:* Lines of data entering and being changed by two boxes labeled AI.

Machine learning systems learn patterns from data to make predictions or decisions. This is what powers spam filters, credit scoring, and medical imaging tools.\
*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: For example, the capital of France is blank space. A resident applying for benefits may need blank space. The two blank spaces are filled in by a machine learning system with Paris and ID.

*\[Visual description]:* Images of a spam email inbox, a credit score meter, and an MRI machine are shown.

Natural language processing (NLP) focuses on enabling computers to understand and work with human language. It underpins voice assistants, translation tools, and speech recognition systems.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: For example. A text message appears “My trash hasn’t been picked up for two weeks on Elm Street.” An AI recognises the Category of this message as Sanitation, the location as Elm Street, and the priority as Medium.

Generative AI is a type of machine learning that is trained on very large datasets of text, images, audio, and code. Rather than just predicting or classifying, these systems also generate new content by modeling patterns in that data.

Tools designed specifically to generate and work with text are known as large language models (LLMs).

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: What is Generative AI?

Generative AI systems differ from traditional software in how they produce results. A calculator or spreadsheet follows fixed rules and will give the same answer every time. By contrast, Generative AI produces outputs probabilistically, based on patterns learned from data. As a result, if you ask the same question more than once, you will often get very similar—but not identical—answers.

*\[Visual description]:* Two CVs are reviewed by a Gen AI, one of the CVs is passed, the other is labeled Fail.

Generative AI systems learn patterns from large amounts of training data, sometimes compared to how humans learn from exposure to language.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Gen AI does not understand meaning or intent. GenAI is limited to pattern recognition and prediction.

But unlike people, they do not understand meaning or intent; they model statistical relationships in data.

Increasingly, these capabilities are being built into tools you already use, such as Microsoft Word, Google Docs, Outlook, and Gmail.

*\[Visual description]: the Logos of Word, Google Docs, Outlook, and Gmail appear.*

Many Generative AI systems are multimodal: you can give instructions in text or by voice, and they can respond with text, speech, images, or even computer code.

*\[Visual description]:* An AI UI is shown, with the option to write a prompt or to enable audio input.

Generative AI tools are not search engines—they do not simply retrieve existing documents. Instead, they generate responses based on patterns learned during training.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Generative AI tools need inputs and outputs, which can be text, audio, images, or videos.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Always verify against a primary source.

Unless a tool is connected to external data sources, its knowledge is limited to its training data and may be out of date. Even tools that search the web may not always retrieve the most current information. For time-sensitive information—such as current policy, recent legislation, or up-to-date statistics—always verify against a primary source.

*\[Visual description]:* An AI tool called perplexity is shown. The user types in the following question: Who was the 38th President of the US. The AI responds by showing Gerald R. Ford. and provides sources from where it gathered the information.

A common question is whether what you type is private. The answer depends on the platform and plan. In general, content entered into free consumer tools may be used to improve the underlying models, whereas paid or enterprise versions typically provide stronger privacy protections and clearer data controls. As a rule, only enter sensitive, confidential, or personally identifiable information if your organization has explicitly approved the use of that tool for such data.
