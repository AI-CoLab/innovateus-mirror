Welcome back. We’ve covered ten common tasks you can do with Generative AI. Now let’s turn to four practical tips for getting started and integrating these tools into your work.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Tip 1: Starts with internal tasks.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Disclaimer: The commercial tools mentioned in this course are for illustrative purposes and do not imply endorsement by InnovateUS.

When you're getting started with GenAI, begin with low-risk internal uses. Help yourself prepare for a meeting, write a first draft of an email to a colleague, or summarize a document for your own use.

*\[Visual description]:* An AI is asked to help prepare a user for a meeting by creating a short briefing with the key issue, questions they should ask, points to discuss, and three possible next steps.

This gives you time to learn how to guide these tools effectively before you put them to work on something the public will see.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Tip 2: Break down big jobs.

GenAI works best on specific, well-defined tasks — not sprawling projects. If you need to develop a new policy, break it into parts: research, drafting, stakeholder outreach, data analysis. Then use GenAI to help with each step along the way.

*\[Visual description]:* The mentioned parts are arranged in a mind map around the goal of developing a new policy.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Tip 3: set realistic expectations.

When applying GenAI to a task, think of it like a brand-new hire. You wouldn't immediately assign a complex project to someone just starting out. You'd start with a task that can be explained quickly and clearly — like proofreading a draft. The more experienced you get at using these tools, the more complex the tasks you can assign.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Tip 4: Experiment, then evaluate.

The more you try these tools, the better you'll get at using them. The best results come from practice and experimentation. Stay in the driver's seat: GenAI is a great helper, so long as you actively evaluate what it produces.
