# Video 3.2 | Summarising and Synthesising

This video explores three of the ten key areas where GenAI can assist: summarizing, labeling, and researching. Hands-on demos and exercises allow participants to practice these applications.

Welcome back! We're discussing ten ways to use GenAI in government work. While our first three tasks were about creating content, let's look at content analysis.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: 4: Summarizing.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Disclaimer: The commercial tools mentioned in this course are for illustrative purposes and do not imply endorsement by InnovateUS.

In government, we often deal with large amounts of information: lengthy PDFs, voluminous regulations,  notes from countless meetings. GenAI excels at distilling it.

*\[Visual description]:* An AI tool is tasked with summarising a multi-page PDF document into 200 words.

You can also generate summaries from audio files. Platforms like Zoom, Teams, Wudpecker, and [Otter.ai](http://Otter.ai) all offer AI-powered transcription and summarization features.

*\[Visual description]:* The Otter AI interface is shown as it transcribes a public meeting from an audio file.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: 5: Categorizing & Labeling.

Sometimes we don't need to summarize but to sort — like when you have a large number of resident comments from a public consultation. GenAI is very good at classifying. You can provide a set of text, give it guidance on the label types you want, and ask it to sort them.

*\[Visual description]:* An AI tool labels a set of public comments by their sentiment.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: 6: Synthesizing Information Across Sources.

Often, you have information from multiple sources and need to extract insights. Try uploading multiple documents and ask for a comparison.

*\[Visual description]:* An AI tool is asked to compare 7 documents and to summarise their overlap.

See you in the next video, where we'll wrap up our ten examples.
