Why do hallucinations happen?

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: What is a hallucination?

Generative AI creates responses based on patterns in language, which means it can produce text that is fluent, confident, and false. This is often called a “hallucination.”

These systems have improved, but the problem has not disappeared. Error rates vary by platform, task, and prompt—and they change as models are updated. When accuracy matters, treat AI output as a starting point and verify it against authoritative sources.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Hallucinations.

What can you do about hallucinations?

First, try more than one tool. If you run the same prompt in different systems, you can compare the answers—like getting a second opinion.

Second, use tools that show their sources. Some systems combine AI with web search and give you citations, which makes it easier to check their claims.

Third, when possible, use tools that are grounded in specific data—like a set of official documents or policies. That can reduce errors, though it doesn’t eliminate them.

Fourth, be clear in how you ask. More specific prompts usually lead to better results.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: a screenshot of the IEEE Spectrum article titled: AI Models Fail Miserably at This One Easy Task: Telling Time.

And finally, check the output. AI models may write a flawless memo but they  cannot reliably tell time

Think of it like reviewing work from a junior colleague—you still need to verify before you rely on it.

When you’re checking AI output, there are a few simple things to look for.

If it cites a study or a statistic, take a moment to find the original source.

Fabricated or misattributed citations do happen. If it names an author, make sure that person actually exists—and that they really wrote what’s being attributed to them.

For important claims, cross-check against a reliable source you trust.

And if the tool gives you a link, click it. AI-generated URLs can look convincing but sometimes lead nowhere.

Finally, you can also ask the tool directly: Are you confident this is accurate? What’s your source? It won’t always fix the problem, but it can surface useful clues.

One practical point: AI outputs can change from one run to the next. For official work, keep a record of what the tool produced and what you changed.
