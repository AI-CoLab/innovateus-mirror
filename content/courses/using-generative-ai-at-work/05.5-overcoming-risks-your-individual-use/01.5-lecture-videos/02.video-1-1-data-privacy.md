# Video 1.1 | Data Privacy

The module addresses the responsible use of GenAI by discussing potential risks and mitigation strategies. Data privacy, hallucinations, and bias are introduced as key challenges. Participants learn to assess and avoid these risks through practical guidance and organizational policies.

### Audio description transcript - Data Privacy

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: The InnovateUS Logo

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Responsible AI for Public Professionals: Understanding Risks and Building Resilience.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Data Privacy.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Ariana Escanlante, presenter, InnovateUS

As with any software or website, the privacy of what you input into GenAI tools is governed by the software's terms of use, which vary by platform and your organization’s contract.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Privacy.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Read the ‘Terms of Use’ and check with legal counsel.

It's always advisable to read those terms and check with legal counsel when in doubt.

As a general rule, free versions of popular GenAI tools may use what you input to train and improve their technology. Assume you have limited privacy on these platforms.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: An AI siphoning data from a user's computer while saying “Feed me your data” and “I’m free, you pay me with your data!”, generated by Canva.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Enterprise Licences.

Enterprise-wide licenses offered by major services often provide greater privacy protection, including limiting the reuse of your data. You should always check with your IT department regarding what's approved in your organization before using any AI tool, and especially before inputting any personally-identifiable information about residents, colleagues, or yourself.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Privacy.

Unless using an approved tool and  your agency policy permits otherwise (many do)

Do not share internal memos, documents not cleared for publication, sensitive data

Do not include personally identifiable information about residents, colleagues, or yourself

Do not share passwords, private keys, or proprietary details

Don't share anything you wouldn't share publicly

One more consideration: even when using an approved AI assistant, in many jurisdictions, your prompts and GenAI outputs may be subject to public records laws, freedom of information law requests, or records retention requirements — just like email or other official communications. Some agencies also require that AI-assisted work products be logged or disclosed.

These tools are excellent for transcribing and summarizing audio recordings. But be mindful that meeting transcriptions may also be subject to retention and disclosure laws.

And, of course, make sure you have consent before recording or transcribing any meeting, especially involving sensitive or confidential topics.
