### Audio description transcript - Deep Fakes, Astroturfing & Private Ownership

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: The InnovateUS Logo.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Responsible AI for Public Professionals: Understanding Risks and Building Resilience.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Deepfakes, Astroturfing and Private Ownership.

Welcome back!

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Deepfakes.

GenAI has the power to produce life-like images and videos. This creates extraordinary opportunity — and serious risk.

*\[Visual description]:* A short David Beckham commercial for Malaria fundraising is shown, generated by AI.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Ariana Escanlante, presenter, InnovateUS.

With the ability to create video from text at minimal cost, this dramatically reduces the price of producing educational content and training videos.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Ariana is not AI, she’s a real person.

This is what the company Synthesia did when it created a malaria awareness campaign with David Beckham — using AI to make the soccer star speak in nine different languages. It's hard to tell the difference from the real thing.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: a screenshot of the Malaria No More case study webpage.

But some are misusing this ability to impersonate real people. In a recent elaborate phishing scheme, bad actors impersonated a company's CFO on a video call and tricked an employee into wiring them $25 million.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: a screenshot of the CNN World article titled: Finance worker pays out $25 million after video call with deepfake ‘chief financial officer.’

It's imperative that public sector organizations understand this technology — so we can use it responsibly and recognize when others use it against us and the residents we serve.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: a screenshot of the Congressional ‘TAKE IT DOWN’ Act. A specific section is highlighted: “Upon receiving such a notice, a covered platform must remove the intimate visual depiction “as soon as possible” but no later than 48 hours after receiving the notice.”

Governments are responding. The TAKE IT DOWN Act — signed into federal law in May 2025 — criminalizes the non-consensual publication of deepfake intimate images and requires platforms to remove flagged content within 48 hours. The first federal conviction under the law came in April 2026. At the state level, almost every state now has deep-fake-related laws, with 64 new laws enacted in 2025 alone. More legislation is expected as the technology becomes more accessible and the harms more visible.

GenAI holds real promise for improving public engagement.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: a screenshot of The Los Angeles Times article titled: Southern California air board rejected pollution rules after flood of comments from AI-powered platform.

But this same technology makes it dramatically easier to flood comment systems with computer-generated submissions — a practice known as astroturfing.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Not exclusive to AI.

With GenAI, bad actors can generate thousands of unique, convincing submissions, making organized manipulation far harder to detect.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Bad actors can generate fake submissions, or even use real names and identities.

More troubling: real people's names and identities are now being used to submit fake comments without their knowledge.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: a screenshot of the Futurism article titled: Barrage of Emails From AI Politics Platform Defeats Clean Air Initiative.

 In Los Angeles, more than 20,000 AI-generated comments opposing a clean air initiative were submitted by a platform billing itself as an "AI-powered grassroots advocacy platform" — with some constituents later reporting they had no idea comments had been filed in their names.

As the volume and sophistication of AI-generated comments grows, agencies will need both technical tools and clear policies to distinguish genuine public input from fabricated submissions — because the integrity of public participation depends on it.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Private Ownership.

Today the GenAI we use in government is controlled by just a handful of companies. These companies are ultimately beholden to shareholders, not the public interest.

This concentration of power is prompting serious debate about what's often called AI sovereignty — the idea that nations and communities should have meaningful control over the AI systems that shape their information, services, and decisions, rather than depending entirely on a small number of private platforms.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: a screenshot of the European Commission press release titled: EU launches InvestAI initiative to mobilise €200 billion of investment in artificial intelligence.

Governments around the world are responding.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: a screenshot of the EU AI Continent Action Plan.

The EU's AI Continent Action Plan commits roughly €200 billion to building European AI infrastructure and launched the OpenEuroLLM initiative to develop open large language models across all 24 official EU languages.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: a screenshot of the OpenEuroLLM website.

India's national AI mission, approved in 2024 with over $1 billion in funding, is investing in domestically developed models that can serve India's 22 languages and public digital infrastructure.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: a screenshot of the Stip Compass website on the India AI Mission. A specific section is highlighted: “to erect a cutting-edge, scalable AI computing infrastructure by deploying over 10,000 Graphics Processing Units (GPUs) through strategic public-private collaborations.”

There is also growing demand for public AI — publicly funded models and infrastructure that operate under principles of transparency and democratic accountability, serving as counterweights to private concentration.

The question is not whether private sector innovation matters — it clearly does — but whether it's sufficient on its own, without public alternatives or democratic oversight, to govern a technology this consequential.

Much of the information we consume and create will increasingly be mediated by a small number of GenAI models. How that power is governed — and by whom — is one of the defining public policy questions of our time.
