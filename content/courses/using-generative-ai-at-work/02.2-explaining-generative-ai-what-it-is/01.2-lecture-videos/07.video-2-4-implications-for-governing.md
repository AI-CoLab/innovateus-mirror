# Video 2.4 | Implications for Governing

This video introduces how GenAI can help create governments that are more conversational, accessible, responsive, and evidence-based. Examples from various jurisdictions illustrate GenAI's potential to improve public service delivery, emphasizing the approach of "AI on tap, not on top."

### Audio description transcript - Implications for Governing

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: The InnovateUS Logo.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Responsible AI for Public Professionals: Using GenAI in Government.

*\[Visual description]:* Title card: Implications for Governing.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Ariana Escanlante, presenter, InnovateUS.

In our last video, we introduced GenAI and showed how it works. So let's briefly reflect on how we can use them to create governments that c-a-r-e:

First, GenAI can help us create a more conversational government — supplying information, benefits, and services around the clock. From Heidelberg, Germany to Prospect Park, NJ — population just over 6,000 — public institutions are turning to AI chatbots to provide 24/7 answers to questions like "What permit do I need to open a café?"

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Disclaimer: The commercial tools mentioned in this course are for illustrative purposes and do not imply endorsement by InnovateUS.

Second, GenAI can help us create a more accessible government. The free AIEP platform translates the government-speak of a child’s individualized education program into plain language — in English and Spanish— so a parent can understand and advocate better for her child.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: the AIEP tool webpage is shown.

Third, GenAI can help governments be more responsive. Projects like the UK's "Analyser" use GenAI to automatically extract patterns and themes from public comments.

*\[Visual description]:* A clip from a UK Analyser AI tool video is shown.

Finally, we can produce more evidence-based policy and services by using Generative AI for research and data analysis. In cities like Boston, staff can ask a plain-language question of an LLM and get an answer based on the city’s open data system.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: the Opendataboston website is shown.

Public workers can be more productive and responsive by using AI to help with rote and time-consuming tasks.
