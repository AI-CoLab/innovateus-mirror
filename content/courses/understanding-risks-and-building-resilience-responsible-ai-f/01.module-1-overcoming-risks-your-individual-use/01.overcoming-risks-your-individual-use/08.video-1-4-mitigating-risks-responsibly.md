Welcome back! Responsible use of GenAI, requires adherence to five crucial behaviors:

Staying current on best practices by learning from others

Critically assessing every output

Maintaining human control…AI on Tap, not on Top

Being transparent and always disclosing your use of GenAI

Prioritizing lower-risk applications

You've already started learning by taking this course!. To keep improving, focus on three things: practice, policy, and peer learning.

Use these tools regularly on real work so you build intuition for what works.

Make sure you understand your organization’s rules on data security and approved tools before using them.

And learn from others—share effective prompts and workflows with colleagues, and adopt what works.

When evaluating outputs, work on tasks within your area of expertise so you can assess quality and accuracy. Ask yourself: could I defend this to a colleague? Use more than one tool to compare results, and never treat GenAI as a substitute for human judgment. For example, don’t publish public-facing content or even send around a draft of a memo without carefully reviewing it first.

Be transparent. Clearly label when content has been created or assisted by AI, whether for internal or public use. Document the tools, prompts, and methods you used so others can understand and replicate your work.

In some cases, accessibility accommodations may affect disclosure requirements, for example, employees using AI-powered assistive tools. Consult your agency's legal counsel or disability services office if you have questions about whether an exemption applies to your situation.

Prioritize low-risk uses. There is less risk in using GenAI to draft internal materials that will be reviewed than in generating content residents rely on for critical decisions. Always build in human oversight.

This is sometimes described as “AI on tap, not on top.” GenAI can support your work, but the responsibility and accountability remain with you—especially for high-stakes outcomes that affect people’s lives.

Transparency also builds trust. Residents increasingly expect to know when AI is used in government services or communications. Clear disclosure helps maintain credibility and allows errors to be identified and corrected.

Remember: there is no such thing as zero risk. The goal is to reduce risk. When evaluating GenAI, compare it to current practice—does it improve accuracy, consistency, or fairness? Human processes are not error-free either, so the question is whether these tools help us do better.

Finally, if your organization hasn't yet approved any AI tools, that's not uncommon. Consider raising the conversation with your supervisor. You can make the case by sharing what you've learned in this course about potential benefits and safeguards needed. Many organizations have started with a limited pilot on a single approved platform before expanding.
