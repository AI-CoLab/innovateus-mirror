# Video 1.3 | Bias

Bias in GenAI is discussed, stemming from biased training data. Examples illustrate how GenAI can replicate societal biases. Techniques to correct for bias, such as targeted prompting and manual correction, are introduced, along with the potential for GenAI to help identify bias.

Welcome back. We’ve looked at hallucinations—now let’s turn to bias.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: A scale leveraging Bias against Facts within a person's head, generated by ChatGPT5.5.

Bias in AI means outputs are not neutral—they can advantage some groups and disadvantage others. In government, that can lead to unequal treatment, flawed decisions, and reduced trust.

The root of this bias lies in the training data. GenAI is trained on vast amounts of text from the internet — the good and the bad.

*\[Visual description]:* Data with “Bias In” written on it enters a visual representation of a Gen AI, and it then outputs data with “Bias Out” written on it.

Generative AI does not create bias from scratch; it learns patterns in the data and can reproduce or amplify them.

For example, when asked to generate or evaluate resumes, a model may produce different responses for otherwise identical candidates based only on names, reflecting patterns of bias found in human decision-making.

*\[Visual description]:* Two CVs are reviewed by a Gen AI, one of the CVs is passed, and the other is labeled Fail.

Even with balanced training data, bias can also arise from how the model generalizes patterns. If training data overrepresents certain pathways to success—such as graduates of a small set of prestigious universities—the model may reflect those patterns even when they are not relevant.

AI researchers are working to reduce bias through techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback, where models are trained to avoid harmful or stereotypical outputs. But because these systems learn from real-world data, bias cannot be fully eliminated.

*\[Visual description]:* Text appears: Disclaimer: The commercial tools mentioned in this course are for illustrative purposes and do not imply endorsement by InnovateUS.

You can sometimes correct for bias directly. If you ask for an image of a lawyer and get only one gender or race, ask for alternatives.

*\[Visual description]:* The AI first generates an image of a white male lawyer. When asked to provide alternatives with different genders and races, it creates four images of lawyers with various racial backgrounds, of which 3 are female.

You can also ask GenAI to review text or images for potential bias, and some tools are designed specifically for that purpose. Still, these are not perfect detectors and require human judgment.

*\[Visual description]:* When asked to generate an image of a doctor without bias on race or gender, the AI provides two images containing a female doctor.

Bias also appears in translation. Quality is generally strong for widely spoken languages, but it can be weaker for less-resourced languages, including many indigenous languages. This can lead to inaccurate or culturally inappropriate results. When translating for these communities, always have a fluent speaker review the output.

Bias is not always easy to detect. Although Generative AI systems can provide explanations, these do not reliably reflect how the model produced its output, making problematic patterns difficult to identify.

*\[Visual description]:* When asked to explain the reasoning behind an answer, the AI does not mention bias in its analysis of its own response.

This is one reason policymakers have placed limits on high-risk uses of AI, especially in areas like employment, education, and access to services.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: a screenshot of the EU Artificial Intelligence Act. Chapter: High-Risk AI Systems.

The takeaway is straightforward: be thoughtful about the tasks you assign to GenAI, and stay in control of decisions that affect people.

*\[Visual description]:* Image appears: a screenshot of the White House paper titled: Accelerating Federal Use of AI through Innovation, Governance, and Public Trust.

At the same time, GenAI can also help identify bias—if used carefully. You can ask it to flag potentially biased language or outputs for you to correct, but the final judgment must remain with you.

In our final video in this section, we'll discuss strategies for mitigating all these risks.
